# Creative Review Rubric

Use this rubric to critique work consistently. Score each dimension from 1 to 5 and include evidence. Scores support discussion; they do not replace professional judgment.

## Rating scale
- **1 — Fails:** Major problems prevent the work meeting the brief.
- **2 — Weak:** Useful elements exist, but important issues remain.
- **3 — Adequate:** Basic requirements are met; improvements remain.
- **4 — Strong:** Well-resolved, coherent, and fit for purpose.
- **5 — Excellent:** Exceptionally clear, controlled, and effective in context.

## Core dimensions
| Dimension | Review question |
|---|---|
| Brief alignment | Does the result solve the stated problem and meet constraints? |
| Concept | Is there a clear, meaningful idea? |
| Hierarchy and clarity | Can the audience understand what matters and what to do? |
| Coherence | Do composition, style, detail, and execution support one another? |
| Craft | Is execution controlled and technically appropriate? |
| Context and usability | Does it work for its audience and real environment? |
| Distinctiveness | Does it have a purposeful point of view rather than arbitrary novelty? |
| Production readiness | Can it be delivered, maintained, reproduced, or built as specified? |

## Discipline-specific checks
**Graphic design:** typography, contrast, grid, alignment, reading order, brand consistency, color behavior, export dimensions, and legibility at actual use size.

**Interior and spatial design:** human scale, circulation, ergonomics, accessibility, lighting, material suitability, maintenance, acoustics, buildability, and review against applicable codes by qualified professionals.

**Video and motion:** story clarity, shot purpose, continuity, pacing, framing, sound intelligibility, motion readability, compositing quality, and delivery specifications.

**AI-assisted production:** prompt and parameter traceability, controllability, consistency, artifact detection, rights and provenance, reproducibility, and human approval.

## Issue format
1. **Observation:** What exactly is visible or measurable?
2. **Impact:** Why does it matter to the brief or audience?
3. **Priority:** Critical / High / Medium / Low.
4. **Correction:** What change would improve it?
5. **Verification:** How will we know the correction worked?

Record strengths worth preserving as well as weaknesses to correct. Do not use generic praise or criticism without evidence.

## Release gate
Do not call a deliverable production-ready until brief constraints and technical specifications are checked, critical defects are resolved or explicitly accepted, required attribution is present, and the user has approved the intended final direction.
